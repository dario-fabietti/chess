# 1200–1400 · Developing club player

[← 1000–1200](elo-1000-1200.md) · [index](README.md) · [next band →](elo-1400-1600.md)

## Player expectations

Rating guides describe this band as playing "chess in the real sense":
one-move blunders are now the exception; a small personal repertoire
exists with theory up to ~move 8–10 in the pet lines; Lucena and Philidor
are recognized by name; tactical vision reaches four-move combinations on
a good day. Losses come from two- to three-move tactics, from strategic
drift after the opening, and from technique — winning positions that don't
get won. This is also where "hope chess" finally dies: the player asks
what the opponent's move threatens on most moves, not just some.

## Depth of thought

Three to five ply, matching Steps 3 (and the entry to Steps 4, which
introduces the **preparatory move** — tactics that need to be set up one
move ahead). Quiet moves inside forcing lines are now visible but
expensive; calculation stops one move too early under pressure
("quiescence errors": ending analysis on their recapture, missing the
follow-up check).

## Tactical ideas to teach

- Second-tier motifs to fluency: **deflection, decoy/attraction, x-ray,
  clearance, interference, trapped piece**.
- **Zwischenzug used offensively**, not just survived.
- Combining two motifs in one sequence (the definition of a combination).
- Defensive tactics: counter-checks, desperado pieces, escaping via
  counterattack.
- Calculation hygiene: list checks/captures/threats for *both* sides at
  the start and at the END of each line.

## Checkmates to know

- **Greek gift (Bxh7+) executed with its conditions checklist**: knight
  reaches g5 with tempo, queen has the h5/d3 route, the defender count on
  the king's field wins, Black's knight can't reach f6/f8 in time.
- Boden's mate (criss-cross bishops), Légal's pattern, hook mate,
  Blackburne's shape.
- Philidor's Legacy (smothered with the queen sacrifice) from real
  positions.
- Building mating nets against a fleeing king — cutting the escape path
  ahead of the checks.

## Openings

Consolidation, not expansion. Guides for the band: main lines 5–8 moves
deep with two or three common deviations covered; the classic bloat trap
is learning five openings shallowly instead of three deeply. Recommended
repertoire skeletons: Italian (adding the slow d3 system), or London with
actual understanding of when e5/c4 breaks come; Caro-Kann or 1...e5 vs
1.e4; QGD or Slav structures vs 1.d4. The Sicilian is explicitly deferred
by multiple sources until ~1600. New habit: after every game, find the
first moment you were out of book and what the plan *should* have been.

## Middlegame & strategic ideas

- First structured planning method: assess (material, king safety,
  activity, pawns) → pick a target → improve pieces toward it.
- Imbalances vocabulary begins (Silman's *Amateur's Mind* territory):
  bishop pair, good vs bad bishop, knight vs bishop by pawn structure.
- Open file technique: seize, double, invade the 7th.
- Attack prerequisites checklist: stable center? more force on that wing?
  lines openable? — pawn storms without these fail here constantly.
- When to trade: ahead → simplify; cramped → trade a defender count.

## Pawn structures

- **IQP from both sides**: the attacker's kit (piece activity, e5 outpost,
  d5 break) vs the defender's kit (blockade, trades, endgame).
- **Carlsbad structure named**, with the minority attack (b4–b5) as the
  model queenside plan.
- Chains: attack the base with the right lever (f6/c5 vs the French
  chain); locked centers point the attack direction.
- Passed pawn creation from a majority; candidate pawn first.

## Typical combinations

- Greek gift with full follow-up; smothered mate with the Qg8+ sac.
- Back-rank combinations with two deflections/overloads.
- "Petite combinaison": two-motif sequences that net a pawn or the
  exchange, not mate.
- Simplifying combinations into technically won endings (now that the
  endings ARE technically won for them).

## Endgames

Silman Class C (1400–1599) is the working set: **Lucena and Philidor**
positions for real; outside passed pawn as a winning method; K+2 doubled
pawns vs K; bishop + wrong rook-pawn draw; opposite-colored bishop drawing
tendencies; minor-piece endgame basics (activity, pawn placement on the
right colors).

## Typical blunders (what the coach watches for)

1. Combinations attempted on excitement without the defender count
   ("unsound sacs").
2. Attacking on the flank while the center is open or opponent-owned.
3. Calculation stopping one forcing move short.
4. Plan drift: five "active" moves with no common target.
5. Won rook endings drawn (rook passive, king late); pawn-up endings
   fumbled.
6. Time trouble self-inflicted by early over-thinking of quiet positions.

## What a good teacher does here

Moves the ratio from "what" to "why": the student annotates their own
games with plans and gets them critiqued; candidate-move discipline is
trained (list 2–3 candidates before calculating any); an endgame technique
curriculum runs in parallel (this is the cheapest 100 points in the band).
Model games in the student's own openings replace opening-line memorizing.
Reviews now legitimately cover two items: the biggest tactical moment and
the biggest planning moment.

## Coach tuning hints (our app)

- Explanations: 4–5 ply; full tactical vocabulary bare; light positional
  terms (outpost, open file, bishop pair) used freely, structural names
  (IQP, Carlsbad) with a one-line gloss.
- Reviews may include one tactical + one positional observation; engine
  deltas in pawns are fine ("this gave back about 0.8").
- When a combination was available and missed, show the first two moves
  and name the motifs, then the punchline — don't dump the full PV.
- Endgame positions: name the theoretical pattern and whether it's won or
  drawn with best play (Lucena/Philidor literacy is expected here).
