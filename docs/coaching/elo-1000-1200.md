# 1000–1200 · Improving amateur

[← 800–1000](elo-0800-1000.md) · [index](README.md) · [next band →](elo-1200-1400.md)

## Player expectations

The "improving amateur" range (community classification: 800–1200). One-
move threats are usually seen; the standard motifs are solved in puzzles;
development and castling happen by habit. What's missing is reliability
under game conditions and any real middlegame direction — plans remain
vague ("attack the king", "develop and see"). Study-plan literature for
this band is unanimous: **tactics are ~70% of the improvement lever** here;
games are still mostly decided by a missed simple tactic, just two moves
deep now instead of one. Time management remains shaky.

## Depth of thought

Two to four ply — the core Steps 2 range. Forced sequences of captures and
checks can be followed four ply out; quiet moves inside a line still break
visualization. The discipline of checking *all* checks and captures (not
the first attractive one) is the growth edge.

## Tactical ideas to teach

- Fluency drills on the full Step-2 motif set until fast and reliable:
  double attack with every piece, pin (absolute vs relative), skewer,
  discovered attack/check, back rank, mate in 2.
- **Elimination of the defense** as a family: capture the defender,
  deflect it, overload it.
- **Overloading** named and practiced; **zwischenzug** taught explicitly
  (first as defense: "before you recapture, check checks").
- Simplifying combinations: trade down into a won pawn ending on purpose.

## Checkmates to know

- Smothered mate executed from typical middlegame positions.
- Anastasia's, Arabian, corridor and hook mates as recognized shapes.
- **Greek gift awareness (defensive)**: what makes Bxh7+ work against you —
  keep a knight able to reach f6/f8 or a bishop watching h7's approach
  squares; don't strip your own king's cover.
- Mating nets: cutting off flight squares before checking.

## Openings

A consistent 1 + 1 + 1 repertoire played every game for months (repertoire
guides: commit for ≥3 months / 50 games; specialists beat generalists at
this level). Depth: main line 5–8 moves with the *plan* attached — e.g.
Italian: c3 + d4 break, or London: e3/Bd3/Nbd2 with the e5 or c4 lever.
Against gambits: accept once, learn why, then choose the safe decline.
After each game, look up only the moment you left your known moves.

## Middlegame & strategic ideas

- Plans get names: open file → rook (then double); outpost → knight;
  opposite-side castling → pawn storm race, same-side → piece pressure.
- The **attackers-vs-defenders audit** before committing to any attack.
- Weak color complexes around the king in embryo: "you traded the bishop
  that guards those squares".
- When the position is quiet: improve the worst piece (the method, not
  just the slogan).

## Pawn structures

- IQP (isolated queen's pawn) met for the first time from the defending
  side: blockade the square in front with a knight, trade minor pieces,
  steer to the endgame.
- Pawn chains: "attack the base" as the first chain concept (French-style
  structures appear via the Caro/French move orders at this level).
- Healthy vs crippled majorities; when doubled pawns are actually fine
  (open file compensation).

## Typical combinations

Three-move combinations built from two motifs: remove the defender →
fork; deflection → back-rank mate; pin → pile-up → win of the pinned
piece; discovered check → grab material with the moving piece. The
sacrifice on f7/f2 followed by a fork or mating attack — calculated, not
hoped.

## Endgames

Silman Class D (1200–1399) previewed, Class E consolidated: opposition
used correctly (not just known); K+P vs K — all the non-rook-pawn wins and
the drawing methods; minor piece vs lone pawn (stopping it, when the piece
must be given); "Fox in the Chicken Coop" and "Deep Freeze" technique
names; counting pawn races with checks along the way.

## Typical blunders (what the coach watches for)

1. Two-move oversights: the reply-to-the-reply is missed.
2. Recapture automatism vs zwischenzug (both suffering it and missing
   their own).
3. Attacks continued after the defender count says stop.
4. Trading into lost pawn endings (or declining trades into won ones).
5. One-result thinking with the clock: fast when winning, frozen when
   worse.
6. Opening leaves the book on move 5 and drifts without a plan.

## What a good teacher does here

Enforces a written thought process (Heisman's Real Chess: opponent's
threat → my checks/captures/threats → pick candidate → blunder-check) and
audits it during reviews. Has the student tag every loss by cause
(tactical miss / time / opening / endgame) so training targets facts, not
feelings. Daily 20–30 puzzles at accuracy pace; weekly annotated own game;
endgame curriculum continues. Rapid games only; begins asking "what was
your plan with this move?" and expecting an answer.

## Coach tuning hints (our app)

- Explanations: 3–4 ply; full basic-motif vocabulary bare; introduce
  "deflection"/"overload" with a short gloss the first time per session.
- Move reviews can now reference the *cause* class ("this lost material to
  a two-move tactic: his bishop was overloaded").
- One main lesson per review still applies — pick the most instructive
  error, not all of them.
- Start attaching plans to praise ("good — that rook belongs on the open
  c-file") and quoting the opponent's best threat by name.
- Numeric evals may appear in parentheses, still translated ("winning a
  pawn (+1.1)").
